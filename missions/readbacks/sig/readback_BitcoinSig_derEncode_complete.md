# Read-back: `BitcoinSig.derEncode_complete`

**Binders and hypothesis.** Universally quantified over a byte string $sig$, with one hypothesis $h$: `isValidSignatureEncoding` $sig$ = true. Unfolded, with $\ell = |sig|$ and $sig[i]$ denoting the byte at index $i$ read as a number in $[0,255]$ (and $0$ if out of range), this means **all** of: $9 \leq \ell \leq 73$; $sig[0] = $ `0x30`; $sig[1] = \ell - 3$; with $lenR = sig[3]$, $5 + lenR < \ell$; with $lenS = sig[5 + lenR]$, $lenR + lenS + 7 = \ell$; $sig[2] = $ `0x02`; $lenR \neq 0$; $sig[4] < $ `0x80`; not ($lenR > 1$ and $sig[4] = 0$ and $sig[5] <$ `0x80`); $sig[lenR + 4] =$ `0x02`; $lenS \neq 0$; $sig[lenR+6] <$ `0x80`; not ($lenS > 1$ and $sig[lenR+6] = 0$ and $sig[lenR+7] <$ `0x80`). The last byte $sig[\ell - 1]$ is unconstrained.

**Conclusion.** There exist byte strings $r$, $s$ and a byte $ht$ such that
$$sig = \text{derEncode}\ r\ s\ ht \quad\land\quad \text{minimalInt}\ r \quad\land\quad \text{minimalInt}\ s \quad\land\quad |r| + |s| \leq 66 .$$

**Unfolding.** `derEncode` $r\,s\,ht$ is the byte string
$$[\,\texttt{0x30},\ (|r|+|s|+4) \bmod 256,\ \texttt{0x02},\ |r| \bmod 256\,] \,\|\, r \,\|\, [\,\texttt{0x02},\ |s| \bmod 256\,] \,\|\, s \,\|\, [\,ht\,],$$
of length $|r| + |s| + 7$. `minimalInt` $v$ is the proposition: $v$ is non-empty, its first byte is $<$ `0x80`, and if $|v| > 1$ and its first byte is $0$ then its second byte is $\geq$ `0x80`. So the conclusion says: $sig$ decomposes as a `0x30` byte, a length byte, a `0x02` tag, a length byte, the bytes $r$, a `0x02` tag, a length byte, the bytes $s$, and one trailing byte $ht$, where the two length bytes are the actual lengths of $r$ and $s$ (reduced mod 256, which is harmless since each is at most 65), the sequence-length byte is $|r|+|s|+4$, both $r$ and $s$ are non-empty with a clear top bit in their first byte and no superfluous leading zero, and together they occupy at most 66 bytes.

**Relationship between hypothesis and conclusion.** The hypothesis bound $\ell \leq 73$ together with $\ell = lenR + lenS + 7$ gives $lenR + lenS \leq 66$, and the hypothesis conditions on $sig[4]$, $sig[5]$, $sig[lenR+6]$, $sig[lenR+7]$ are exactly the `minimalInt` conditions on $r = sig[4 .. 4 + lenR)$ and $s = sig[lenR + 6 .. lenR + 6 + lenS)$. The theorem is thus the "completeness" direction: every string accepted by the strict encoding check has the `derEncode` shape with minimal integers.

**What is not said.** The statement does not say the witnesses $r$, $s$, $ht$ are unique (they are determined by $sig$, but uniqueness is not asserted). It does not bound $|r|$ or $|s|$ individually (e.g. it allows $|r| = 65$, $|s| = 1$); only the sum is bounded. It does not say that $r$ or $s$, read as big-endian integers, are below $n$ or non-zero as numbers (e.g. $r = [0]$ satisfies `minimalInt`). It does not say anything about $ht$ (any byte). It does not relate $sig$ to the lax parser `parseDerLax`, to `checkLowS`, or to signature verification. It does not say the converse (that every `derEncode` of minimal integers is accepted) — that is a separate statement. It says nothing about strings for which the encoding check fails.

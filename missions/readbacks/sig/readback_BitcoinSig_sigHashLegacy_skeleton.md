# Read-back: `BitcoinSig.sigHashLegacy_skeleton`

**Binders and hypothesis.** Universally quantified over a byte string $scriptCode$, two transactions $tx$ and $tx'$, and natural numbers $nIn$ and $hashType$ (unbounded). One hypothesis $h$: `sameSkeleton` $tx\,tx'$, which unfolds to the conjunction
$$tx'.\text{version} = tx.\text{version}\ \land\ tx'.\text{vout} = tx.\text{vout}\ \land\ tx'.\text{nLockTime} = tx.\text{nLockTime}\ \land\ \big[(i.\text{prevout}, i.\text{nSequence})\big]_{i \in tx'.\text{vin}} = \big[(i.\text{prevout}, i.\text{nSequence})\big]_{i \in tx.\text{vin}},$$
the last conjunct being equality of the two lists obtained by replacing each input by the pair (prevout, sequence number). It therefore forces $|tx'.\text{vin}| = |tx.\text{vin}|$ and position-wise agreement of prevouts and sequences, while allowing every input's `scriptSig` and `witness` to differ arbitrarily.

**Conclusion.**
$$\text{sigHashLegacy}\ scriptCode\ tx'\ nIn\ hashType = \text{sigHashLegacy}\ scriptCode\ tx\ nIn\ hashType .$$

**Unfolding.** `sigHashLegacy` $sc\,t\,nIn\,ht$ returns `uint256One` (byte 1 then 31 zero bytes) when $ht \mathbin{\&} 31 = 3$ and $|t.\text{vout}| \leq nIn$ — a condition that depends only on the output list, equated by $h$ — and otherwise the double SHA-256 of `serSigTx` $t\,sc\,nIn\,ht$ followed by the 4-byte little-endian $ht$. `serSigTx` reads: $t.\text{version}$ (4 bytes); the input count, which is $1$ if the ANYONECANPAY bit ($128$) of $ht$ is set, else $|t.\text{vin}|$; for each serialized input index $k$ (overridden to $nIn$ under ANYONECANPAY): the prevout of input $k$ (or of the default input $\langle\langle[\,],0\rangle,[\,],0,[\,]\rangle$ if out of range), then `serScriptCode` $sc$ if $k = nIn$ else the single byte `0x00`, then the 4-byte sequence of input $k$ — replaced by 4 zero bytes if $k \neq nIn$ and the low five bits of $ht$ are $2$ (NONE) or $3$ (SINGLE); the output count ($0$ for NONE, $nIn + 1$ for SINGLE, else $|t.\text{vout}|$) and the corresponding `serTxOut` records (value as 8-byte two's complement, then the script with compact-size prefix; $\langle -1,[\,]\rangle$ for non-$nIn$ outputs in SINGLE mode and for out-of-range indices); and $t.\text{nLockTime}$ (4 bytes). The scriptSig and witness fields of inputs are never read. Since $h$ equates every field that is read, position by position, the two byte strings and hence the two hashes agree.

**What is not said.** The statement does not say the converse (equal hashes do not imply `sameSkeleton`). It does not say the hash is independent of $scriptCode$, $nIn$ or $hashType$ — these are held fixed. It does not say that scriptSigs or witnesses are *the only* fields that may differ; it says that *if* everything in the skeleton agrees then the hashes agree. It does not bound $hashType$ or $nIn$. It does not address the `uint256One` special case specially: both sides merely agree on whether they hit it. It does not say anything about the BIP143 hash, nor about signature verification.

# Read-back: `BitcoinSer.header_roundtrip`

**Binders and hypothesis.** A header record $h$, a byte string $\texttt{rest}$ (arbitrary), and $hw : h.\texttt{wf}$, which is the conjunction
$$-2^{31} \le h.\texttt{version} < 2^{31},\quad |h.\texttt{prevBlock}| = 32,\quad |h.\texttt{merkleRoot}| = 32,\quad h.\texttt{time} < 2^{32},\quad h.\texttt{bits} < 2^{32},\quad h.\texttt{nonce} < 2^{32}.$$

**Conclusion.** Parsing the serialized header followed by $\texttt{rest}$ succeeds and returns exactly $h$ with remainder $\texttt{rest}$:
$$\texttt{readHeader}\big(\texttt{serHeader}(h) \mathbin{+\!\!+} \texttt{rest}\big) = \mathrm{some}\,(h, \texttt{rest}).$$
This is an equality of optional pairs; it asserts success, equality of every field of the parsed record with the corresponding field of $h$ (record equality is field-wise), and that the unconsumed suffix is $\texttt{rest}$.

**Unfolding.** The serialization is $\texttt{serInt32}(\texttt{version}) \mathbin{+\!\!+} \texttt{prevBlock} \mathbin{+\!\!+} \texttt{merkleRoot} \mathbin{+\!\!+} \texttt{ser32}(\texttt{time}) \mathbin{+\!\!+} \texttt{ser32}(\texttt{bits}) \mathbin{+\!\!+} \texttt{ser32}(\texttt{nonce})$, where $\texttt{serInt32}(v)$ is the 4 little-endian bytes of $v \bmod 2^{32}$ taken in $[0, 2^{32})$ (two's complement for negative $v$) and $\texttt{ser32}$ is 4 little-endian bytes of a natural number mod $2^{32}$. The parser $\texttt{readHeader}$ performs, in order: $\texttt{readInt32}$ (4 bytes little-endian $u$, returned as $u$ if $u < 2^{31}$, else $u - 2^{32}$); $\texttt{readHash}$ twice (each takes exactly 32 bytes, failing if fewer remain); then $\texttt{readLE}\;4$ three times (unsigned). Each step fails on short input and the whole parse then fails.

The hypotheses are used as follows: the version range guarantees that the signed decode of the two's-complement bytes gives back exactly $h.\texttt{version}$ (for $v$ outside $[-2^{31}, 2^{31})$ the wrap-around would produce a different integer); the two length equalities guarantee that the 32 bytes taken by each $\texttt{readHash}$ are exactly the field (if $\texttt{prevBlock}$ were shorter or longer, the parser would still take 32 bytes but they would straddle fields); and the three $< 2^{32}$ bounds guarantee that the truncating $\texttt{ser32}$ loses nothing so $\texttt{readLE}\;4$ recovers the value. The hypothesis is therefore exactly matched to what the round trip needs — every conjunct is used.

**What is not said.** Nothing for headers violating $\texttt{wf}$: e.g. with a 31-byte $\texttt{prevBlock}$ the parse may still succeed but return a different record, and the theorem is silent. The converse direction ("every successful $\texttt{readHeader}$ comes from $\texttt{serHeader}$ of a well-formed header", or that parsed headers are well-formed) is not stated. Nothing about $\texttt{headerHash}$, about 80-byte length (that is `serHeader_length`), about blocks, or about failure on short input. It is the one-directional parse-after-serialize statement, under full $\texttt{wf}$, with an arbitrary trailing suffix.

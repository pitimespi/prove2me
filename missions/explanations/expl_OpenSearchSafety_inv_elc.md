Strong induction on the term `t` (`Nat.strongRecOn`), after deriving antisymmetry of descent and that the term does not decrease along a descent. From the induction hypothesis the proof derives leader completeness below `t` (for a publication of a term below `t`, take its first publication of the term, `inv_reign_first`, whose election record `pub_election` provides, and apply the hypothesis) and the comparability of committed keys of terms below `t` (leader completeness for different terms, `pub_same_term` for equal terms). A step called `finish` shows that a committed key `C'` of term below `t` to which a voter `m` of `J` sent a publish response is an ancestor of the base: `m`'s term-`t` join is `Below` the base, `join_after_response` bounds it below by `C'`, so the base has term at least 1 and is a publication, and then leader completeness below `t` (if `C'` is of an older term) or `pub_same_term` with the version order (if the terms are equal) places `C'` below the base. Then apply `inv_pivot` to the base. (I) With a committed pivot `D`, compare `D` with the committed key: if the key is below `D`, conclude by transitivity; otherwise take the first committed key `C'` after `D` on the way to it (`inv_first_committed_after`); `inv_commit_quorum_after` gives responders to `C'` forming a quorum of `D`'s last accepted configuration, of which `J` is also a quorum (it is `x` or `y`); `quorum_intersect` gives a common voter, `finish` puts `C'` below the base, and maximality of `D` then puts `C'` below `D`, contradicting antisymmetry. (II) Without a committed ancestor of the base, take the first committed key `C'` of the committed key's branch (`inv_first_committed`); `inv_commit_quorum_first` gives responders forming a quorum of a bootstrap configuration, `J` is a quorum of the bootstrap configuration `x`, `P.InitCompatible` gives a common voter, and `finish` makes `C'` a committed ancestor of the base, a contradiction.

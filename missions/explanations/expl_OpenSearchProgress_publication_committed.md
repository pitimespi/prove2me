The private lemma `commit_track`, an induction over the stable execution, shows that at every point either `(t, s.version)` is committed or some member of `Q` is not among the cluster-manager's publish votes. Initially the votes are empty (`hpv`) and `Q` is nonempty, a quorum never being the empty list (`ne_nil_of_hasQuorum`). A commit, once sent, stays sent. Only the cluster-manager's own `handlePublishResponse` changes its publish votes; if the response it handles makes the votes contain all of `Q`, then by `pub_c` its last committed configuration is one of the four configurations of `hquorum` and its last published configuration is `s`'s accepted one, so the votes are a publish quorum (`pub_quorum`, through `quorum_mono`) and the handler returns the commit (`respond_quorum`) for the response's term and version, which are the cluster-manager's term `t` and last published version `s.version`; that commit is sent. With `hacks` the second alternative is impossible. The hypothesis `hQ` is not used by the proof.

## The claim

When an OpenSearch cluster has no cluster-manager, every cluster-manager-eligible node in candidate mode that has discovered enough peers to form an election quorum (or has just bootstrapped the cluster) starts an election scheduler (`ElectionSchedulerFactory`), which runs a pre-voting round at random intervals. Attempt `k` fires `toPositiveLongAtMost(random.nextLong(), maxDelay(k))` milliseconds after it is scheduled, plus a grace period, where `maxDelay(k) = min(max_timeout, initial_timeout + k · back_off_time)` (defaults 10 s, 100 ms and 100 ms) and `toPositiveLongAtMost(r, M) = nonNegative(r) mod M + 1`; the scheduler's documentation calls the delay uniform in `(0, maxDelay(k)]`. Two candidates that fire at nearly the same time can split the votes and both fail; the randomization makes it likely that one candidate fires well before every other and runs alone. The pre-voting mission proved the delay bounds for every drawn number. This mission proves the probabilistic claim behind the randomization, for draws uniform over the `2^64` Java longs: the exact distribution of a delay, and that a round of competing timers is contested with small probability, and many rounds in a row with exponentially small probability, whatever the timers' offsets and even when an adversary scheduling the network chooses them in reaction to earlier rounds.

## What is proved

A round has `n` candidates; candidate `i` fires at `offset i + randomDelay (bound i) (x i)`, where `x i` is its draw of `random.nextLong()`, `bound i` its attempt's `maxDelay` and `offset i` when its timer was started plus the grace period. A round is *uncontested with margin `δ`* when some candidate fires at least `δ` milliseconds before every other. Probabilities are numbers of draws divided by the number of all draws.

- **The distribution of a delay** (`randomDelay_range`, `randomDelay_card`, `randomDelay_near_uniform`). For a bound `1 ≤ M ≤ 2^63` every delay lies in `[1, M]`, and the delay `d` is produced by exactly `2(⌊(2^63 − d)/M⌋ + 1)` of the `2^64` longs. Writing `2^63 = qM + r` with `0 ≤ r < M`, the delays `1, …, r` have probability `(q + 1)/2^63` and the delays `r + 1, …, M` probability `q/2^63`: uniform up to a relative error of `M/2^63`, about `10^-15` for 10 seconds, and exactly uniform only when `M` divides `2^63`.
- **Two timers** (`close_pair_prob`). Whatever their offsets, and whatever the first timer's bound, two candidates whose second bound is at least `Mmin` fire less than `δ` apart with probability at most `(2δ − 1) · ⌈2^63/Mmin⌉ / 2^63`, about `(2δ − 1)/Mmin`.
- **One round** (`contested_prob_le`). For `n` candidates with arbitrary offsets and bounds between `Mmin` and `2^63`, the round fails to be uncontested with margin `δ` with probability at most `contestedBound n δ Mmin = C(n, 2) · (2δ − 1) · ⌈2^63/Mmin⌉ / 2^63`: in a contested round the first candidate to fire has a follower less than `δ` behind, and the union bound runs over the `C(n, 2)` pairs.
- **Several rounds, against an adaptive scheduler** (`all_contested_prob_le`, the goal). Over `K` rounds whose offsets and bounds are any functions of the draws of the earlier rounds, with every bound between `Mmin` and `2^63`, all `K` rounds are contested with probability at most `contestedBound n δ Mmin ^ K`. An adversary that reacts to what has happened, but cannot foresee future draws, cannot push the probability of `K` contested rounds above the `K`-th power of the per-round bound.
- **The default settings** (`default_example`). With every bound at the default `max_timeout` of 10 seconds (every attempt from attempt 99 on, counting from 0, with the default initial timeout and back-off time of 100 ms), three candidates and a margin of 100 ms, a round is contested with probability below 6% (the bound is about 5.97%), and five consecutive rounds with probability below one in a million (about `7.6 · 10^-7`).

## What this rests on and what it does not say

The Java source at the pinned revision — [`ElectionSchedulerFactory.java`](https://github.com/opensearch-project/OpenSearch/blob/b2a54ba5c0defba3d1d20423e911d4bf920bab19/server/src/main/java/org/opensearch/cluster/coordination/ElectionSchedulerFactory.java), [`Coordinator.java`](https://github.com/opensearch-project/OpenSearch/blob/b2a54ba5c0defba3d1d20423e911d4bf920bab19/server/src/main/java/org/opensearch/cluster/coordination/Coordinator.java) (`startElectionScheduler`), [`DiscoveryModule.java`](https://github.com/opensearch-project/OpenSearch/blob/b2a54ba5c0defba3d1d20423e911d4bf920bab19/server/src/main/java/org/opensearch/discovery/DiscoveryModule.java#L213) — and the pre-voting mission's transcription of `nonNegative` and `toPositiveLongAtMost`, whose definitions are imported. The probabilities are exact counts over finite sets, computed with Mathlib's finite sums and rational numbers.

- **The randomness is idealized.** The draws are uniform over all `2^64` longs and independent across nodes and attempts. In Java each node's scheduler draws from the node's own `java.util.Random`, created as `new Random(Randomness.get().nextLong())`. `java.util.Random` is a 48-bit linear congruential generator: `nextLong()` takes at most `2^48` of the `2^64` values, one node's successive draws are successive outputs of one generator (which the `Coordinator` also uses to pick a node to abdicate to), and nothing is modelled about how the seeds of different nodes relate.
- **Rounds are an abstraction.** Grouping competing timers into rounds, each with its own offsets and bounds, abstracts the scheduler's timeline; the adversary chooses offsets and bounds, not message delays, and which candidates compete in a round is not modelled.
- **The union bound is crude.** The bound grows like `C(n, 2) · (2δ − 1)/Mmin` and exceeds 1, saying nothing, for many candidates or a large margin: with a 10-second bound and a 100 ms margin from 11 candidates on, and with 3 candidates from a margin of 1668 ms on. It needs every bound to be at least `Mmin`; the early attempts have small bounds (100 ms for the first attempt with the default settings), for which it says nothing at a 100 ms margin.
- **An uncontested round is not yet a successful election.** That the candidate firing `δ` ahead wins requires its pre-voting round and its election to complete within `δ`, with nothing else disrupting them: a stable election in the sense of the progress mission, which proves that such an election succeeds. That an uncontested round yields such a period is an informal argument, about timing the model does not have.

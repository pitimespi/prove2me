## The claim

When an OpenSearch cluster has no cluster-manager, every cluster-manager-eligible node in candidate mode that has discovered enough peers to form an election quorum (or has just bootstrapped the cluster) starts an election scheduler (`ElectionSchedulerFactory`), which fires at random intervals; each firing starts a new pre-voting round if the node is still a candidate that may win the election, is healthy and is commissioned. Attempt `k` fires `toPositiveLongAtMost(random.nextLong(), maxDelay(k))` milliseconds after it is scheduled, plus a grace period, where `maxDelay(k) = min(max_timeout, initial_timeout + k · back_off_time)` (defaults 10 s, 100 ms and 100 ms) and `toPositiveLongAtMost(r, M) = nonNegative(r) mod M + 1`; the scheduler's documentation calls the delay uniform in `(0, maxDelay(k)]`. Two candidates that fire at nearly the same time can split the votes and both fail; the randomization is meant to make it likely that one candidate fires well before every other and, if its pre-voting round and election complete within that head start (timing not modelled here), runs alone. The pre-voting mission proved the delay bounds for every drawn number. This mission proves the probabilistic claim behind the randomization, for independent draws uniform over the `2^64` Java longs. It proves the exact distribution of a delay. It proves that a round of `n ≥ 1` competing timers whose bounds all lie between `Mmin ≥ 1` and `2^63` is contested with margin `δ ≥ 1` with probability at most about `C(n, 2) · (2δ − 1)/Mmin`, which is small only when `Mmin` is large compared with `C(n, 2) · (2δ − 1)` (for example for three candidates and a 100 ms margin, once every competing attempt has reached the 10-second maximum timeout). And it proves that `K` rounds are all contested with probability at most the `K`-th power of that bound, whatever the timers' offsets, even when an adversary scheduling the network chooses the offsets and bounds in reaction to the earlier rounds' draws.

## What is proved

A round has `n` candidates; candidate `i` fires at `offset i + randomDelay (bound i) (x i)`, where `x i` is its draw of `random.nextLong()`. `bound i` stands for its attempt's `maxDelay` and `offset i` for when its timer was started plus the grace period, but this reading is informal: in the Lean statements offsets and bounds are free parameters, not linked to `maxDelay`, `SchedulerSettings` or a grace period. A round is *uncontested with margin `δ`* when some candidate fires at least `δ` milliseconds before every other. Probabilities are numbers of draws divided by the number of all draws.

- **The distribution of a delay** (`randomDelay_range`, `randomDelay_card`, `randomDelay_near_uniform`). For a bound `M ≥ 1` every delay lies in `[1, M]`, and for `1 ≤ M ≤ 2^63` each delay `d` with `1 ≤ d ≤ M` is produced by exactly `2(⌊(2^63 − d)/M⌋ + 1)` of the `2^64` longs. Writing `2^63 = qM + r` with `0 ≤ r < M`, the delays `1, …, r` have probability `(q + 1)/2^63` and the delays `r + 1, …, M` probability `q/2^63`: uniform up to a relative error of `M/2^63`, about `10^-15` for 10 seconds, and exactly uniform only when `M` divides `2^63`.
- **Two timers** (`close_pair_prob`). Whatever their offsets, and whatever the first timer's bound (nothing is assumed about it), two candidates whose second bound lies between `Mmin` and `2^63`, with `Mmin ≥ 1`, fire less than `δ ≥ 1` apart with probability at most `(2δ − 1) · ⌈2^63/Mmin⌉ / 2^63`, about `(2δ − 1)/Mmin`.
- **One round** (`contested_prob_le`). For `n ≥ 1` candidates with arbitrary offsets and bounds between `Mmin` and `2^63`, where `Mmin ≥ 1`, the round fails to be uncontested with margin `δ ≥ 1` with probability at most `contestedBound n δ Mmin = C(n, 2) · (2δ − 1) · ⌈2^63/Mmin⌉ / 2^63`: in a contested round the first candidate to fire has a follower less than `δ` behind, and the union bound runs over the `C(n, 2)` pairs.
- **Several rounds, against an adaptive scheduler** (`all_contested_prob_le`, the goal). Over `K` rounds of the same `n ≥ 1` candidates, each drawing afresh in every round, whose offsets and bounds are any functions of the draws of the earlier rounds, with every bound between `Mmin ≥ 1` and `2^63` and a margin `δ ≥ 1`, all `K` rounds are contested with probability at most `contestedBound n δ Mmin ^ K`. An adversary that reacts to the draws of the earlier rounds, but not to those of the current round or later ones, cannot push the probability of `K` contested rounds above the `K`-th power of the per-round bound.
- **The default maximum timeout** (`default_example`, arithmetic on `contestedBound 3 100 10000`, combined here with `contested_prob_le` and `all_contested_prob_le`). With every bound at the default `max_timeout` of 10 seconds (every attempt from attempt 99 on, counting from 0, with the default initial timeout and back-off time of 100 ms), three candidates and a margin of 100 ms (an illustrative choice: nothing here shows that 100 ms suffices for a pre-voting round and an election), a round is contested with probability below 6% (the bound is about 5.97%), and five consecutive rounds with probability below one in a million (about `7.6 · 10^-7`). For earlier attempts the bound is weaker, and it says nothing while the smallest competing bound is at most 597 ms.

## What this rests on and what it does not say

The Java source at the pinned revision — [`ElectionSchedulerFactory.java`](https://github.com/opensearch-project/OpenSearch/blob/b2a54ba5c0defba3d1d20423e911d4bf920bab19/server/src/main/java/org/opensearch/cluster/coordination/ElectionSchedulerFactory.java), [`Coordinator.java`](https://github.com/opensearch-project/OpenSearch/blob/b2a54ba5c0defba3d1d20423e911d4bf920bab19/server/src/main/java/org/opensearch/cluster/coordination/Coordinator.java) (`startElectionScheduler`), [`DiscoveryModule.java`](https://github.com/opensearch-project/OpenSearch/blob/b2a54ba5c0defba3d1d20423e911d4bf920bab19/server/src/main/java/org/opensearch/discovery/DiscoveryModule.java#L213) — and the pre-voting mission's transcription of `nonNegative` and `toPositiveLongAtMost`, whose definitions are imported. The probabilities are exact counts over finite sets, computed with Mathlib's finite sums and rational numbers.

- **The randomness is idealized.** The draws are uniform over all `2^64` longs and independent across nodes and attempts. In Java each node's scheduler draws from the node's own `java.util.Random`, created as `new Random(Randomness.get().nextLong())`. `java.util.Random` is a 48-bit linear congruential generator: `nextLong()` takes at most `2^48` of the `2^64` values, one node's successive draws are successive outputs of one generator (which the `Coordinator` also uses to pick a node to abdicate to), and nothing is modelled about how the seeds of different nodes relate.
- **Rounds are an abstraction.** Grouping competing timers into rounds, each with its own offsets and bounds, abstracts the scheduler's timeline. The adversary chooses offsets and bounds, not message delays, as a deterministic function of the earlier rounds' draws, so it cannot react to the draws of the current round. Every round has the same `n` candidates, each drawing afresh (a candidate that sits a round out can only be represented by a very large offset); the rounds need not be ordered in time, and nothing ties a round to the scheduler's attempt number.
- **The union bound is crude.** The bound grows like `C(n, 2) · (2δ − 1)/Mmin` and exceeds 1, saying nothing, for many candidates or a large margin: with a 10-second bound and a 100 ms margin from 11 candidates on, and with 3 candidates from a margin of 1668 ms on. It needs every bound to be at least `Mmin`; the early attempts have small bounds (100 ms for the first attempt with the default settings), for which it says nothing at a 100 ms margin.
- **An uncontested round is not yet a successful election.** That the candidate firing `δ` ahead wins requires its pre-voting round and its election to complete within `δ`, with nothing else disrupting them: a stable election in the sense of the progress mission, which proves that such an election succeeds. That an uncontested round yields such a period is an informal argument, about timing the model does not have.

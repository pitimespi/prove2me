## The claim

In OpenSearch's cluster coordination a node starts an election by sending `StartJoinRequest`s for a new term, and every node that handles one moves to that term (`CoordinationState.handleStartJoin`, transcribed in the per-node mission) and discards its election and publication state: a leader that handles one is no longer leader. A node that started elections whenever it lost sight of the leader — on the wrong side of a network partition, or while restarting — would depose a leader that the rest of the cluster still follows. OpenSearch therefore precedes every election with a **pre-voting round** (`PreVoteCollector`), a check comparable to the Pre-Vote phase of Raft: the node asks the nodes it has discovered, itself included, whether they would support it, without asking anyone to change its term (a leader whose current term is below the request's still moves to a higher term and starts an election, see below), and starts the election only if the answers it counts form an election quorum.

A node answers a pre-vote request with its current term and the term and version of its last accepted cluster state (the metadata version, for a term-0 state), unless it is unhealthy or knows a leader other than the requester. The requester ignores answers fresher than its own last accepted state — the comparison `handleJoin` makes when it counts votes — and as soon as the senders of the answers it has kept form an election quorum of the last committed and last accepted configurations of its accepted state, it calls `startElection`, once per round. `startElection` sends `StartJoinRequest`s for the term `max(currentTerm, maxTermSeen) + 1`, where `maxTermSeen` is the largest term the node has seen, in pre-vote answers among other messages. Pre-voting rounds are started by an **election scheduler** at random intervals whose bound backs off linearly up to a maximum.

This mission transcribes the pre-vote messages and handlers, the pre-voting round, the term of the election it triggers and the scheduler's delays, at OpenSearch revision `b2a54ba5c0defba3d1d20423e911d4bf920bab19`, on top of the per-node `CoordinationState` mission, and proves what a single round guarantees.

## The transcription

- **Answering** (`handlePreVoteRequest`, PVC:133-163). A node's collector state is its known leader and its own response, both as last set by the `Coordinator`: itself when it becomes leader, the leader it follows, nobody when it becomes a candidate (CO:516, 653, 777, 801, 839, 1157). In Java the response is `getPreVoteResponse()` of its coordination state, transcribed as `preVoteResponseOf` (CO:873-879); `CollectorState` keeps it as a free field, and `no_disruption` holds for every collector state. Health is a Boolean parameter.
- **Rounds** (`PreVotingRound`, PVC:175-300). A round holds the requester's accepted state when it started, the requester, the recorded (sender, response) pairs, and the flags `electionStarted` and `isClosed`; in every reachable round there is at most one pair per sender, a sender being a node value (id and role), as `round_entries` proves (the type itself allows repeats). `handlePreVoteResponse` follows PVC:225-279 step by step and returns, besides the new round, whether `startElection` is called and the term passed to `updateMaxTermSeen`. The requester's own current response, which Java reads when each answer arrives, is a parameter of each step. `RoundReachable es Delivered` stands for the network: it collects the rounds obtained by handling, in any order and any number of times, the responses that the predicate `Delivered` allows, and by closing.
- **The election.** `startElectionTerm` is the term `startElection` uses (CO:593). `handleJoins` handles a list of joins in order with the per-node `handleJoin`, a rejected join leaving the state unchanged, as `Coordinator.handleJoin` (CO:1267-1299) does for joins whose term is not above the node's current term; a join of a higher term is simply rejected, where the `Coordinator` would first move the node to that term (CO:1269).
- **The scheduler** (`ElectionSchedulerFactory`). The four settings with their ranges (ESF:85-115) and the check `initial_timeout ≤ max_timeout` (ESF:133-143); the bound `maxDelay s k = min(maxTimeout, initialTimeout + k · backoffTime)` of attempt `k` (ESF:203); and its delay, `nonNegative(r) mod maxDelay s k + 1` plus the grace period, for the random long `r` (ESF:159-172, 204). In Java the grace period is 0 for the first attempt (CO:1574) and `cluster.election.duration` for the later ones (ESF:218); the transcription takes it as a parameter (the setting `duration` occurs only in `Valid`), and `attemptDelay_bounds` holds for every grace period.

## What is proved

- **The handlers** (`handlePreVoteRequest_spec`, `votes_mem`, `round_entries`, `round_start_quorum`, `round_starts_once`). A node answers exactly when it is healthy and knows no leader or knows the requester as its leader, and then with its own response. A round counts exactly its cluster-manager-eligible senders; it records only delivered responses that are no fresher than its accepted state, one per sender; it calls `startElection` only while open, only when its votes, once the new response is recorded, form an election quorum, and at most once.

- **Pre-voting protects a stable leader** (`no_disruption`). Let a quorum `L` of the last committed or of the last accepted configuration of a node `c`'s accepted state consist of nodes that are unhealthy or follow a leader other than `c`. Then no pre-voting round of `c` over that state starts an election, whatever answers it receives, in whatever order and number, and whatever the election strategy: the nodes of `L` do not answer, every recorded sender answered, and a set of voters disjoint from a quorum of a configuration is not a quorum of it. A node cut off from a cluster whose majority follows a leader cannot depose that leader by starting elections when it comes back.

  The theorem is about the round, not about every effect of the request. Handling a pre-vote request raises the receiver's `maxTermSeen` to at least the requester's current term, and a leader whose current term is below it moves to that term and runs a new election itself, at once or when its publication in progress ends (CO:560-581, CO:1784). Pre-voting stops a returning node from starting elections; it does not stop this term bump when the returning node's term is higher than the leader's.

- **A successful pre-vote predicts a successful election** (`preVote_then_election`). If a round of node `c` over its accepted state has started an election, the election `startElection` begins succeeds, provided nothing changes under it: every node whose answer the round recorded accepts `c`'s `StartJoinRequest` for `T = max(currentTerm, maxTermSeen) + 1`, since its current term is at most `maxTermSeen`, below `T`; and when the joins those nodes send back reach `c`, `c` counts every one of them, so its join votes contain the round's votes, a quorum of both configurations, and it wins. The hypotheses make "nothing changes" precise:
  - the senders have not changed since answering: when a sender handles the `StartJoinRequest`, its current term and its last accepted term and version are those it reported (the round's delivery predicate);
  - `c` has not changed: the round is over `c`'s current accepted state, and after its own start-join `c` handles only joins — no other start-join, publication or restart;
  - no join of a higher term arrives: every join `c` handles has a term at most `T`, because `Coordinator.handleJoin` would first move `c` to the higher term and discard its votes (CO:1269); joins of term at most `T` from other nodes may be interleaved in any order, accepted or rejected;
  - the election strategy is the default one, and `maxTermSeen` is at least the current term of every recorded answer (as `updateMaxTermSeen` ensures, PVC:231).

  The freshness check that `handleJoin` makes at `c` is the one the round already made on the same values; the term check passes because every join is for `T`, `c`'s new term; the remaining checks pass because `c` has just handled its own start-join and its configurations, having quorums, are nonempty.

- **The election scheduler** (`attemptDelay_bounds`, `maxDelay_schedule`, `nonNegative_two_to_one`). For valid settings, attempt `k` waits between `g + 1` and `g + maxDelay s k` milliseconds, `g` its grace period, and the bound is at least 1, so the Java assertion `0 < upperBound` (ESF:170) always holds. The bound starts at `initial_timeout`, grows by `back_off_time` per attempt and stays at `max_timeout` once it reaches it (at the latest from attempt number `max_timeout` on). `nonNegative` maps the Java longs exactly two-to-one onto `[0, 2^63)`, the counting fact behind the uniformity of the random delay.

## What this rests on and what it does not say

The Java source at the pinned revision — [`PreVoteCollector.java`](https://github.com/opensearch-project/OpenSearch/blob/b2a54ba5c0defba3d1d20423e911d4bf920bab19/server/src/main/java/org/opensearch/cluster/coordination/PreVoteCollector.java), [`Coordinator.java`](https://github.com/opensearch-project/OpenSearch/blob/b2a54ba5c0defba3d1d20423e911d4bf920bab19/server/src/main/java/org/opensearch/cluster/coordination/Coordinator.java) (`getPreVoteResponse`, `startElection`, `updateMaxTermSeen`, `handleJoin`, `startElectionScheduler`), [`ElectionSchedulerFactory.java`](https://github.com/opensearch-project/OpenSearch/blob/b2a54ba5c0defba3d1d20423e911d4bf920bab19/server/src/main/java/org/opensearch/cluster/coordination/ElectionSchedulerFactory.java) — and the per-node mission's transcription of `CoordinationState`. Only that mission's definitions are imported.

- The `Coordinator`'s side effects are outside the transcribed functions: the term bump above, the sending of messages, and the transitions between candidate, leader and follower. `nodeMayWinElection`, which `startElection` checks, is transcribed but not used.
- Nodes are compared by id and role. Java compares ephemeral ids, which change when a node restarts, so the two differ only between two incarnations of one node: a follower of a node's previous incarnation refuses its pre-vote request in Java and answers it here.
- The election strategy sees voter ids, as in the per-node mission; `no_disruption` holds for every strategy, `preVote_then_election` for the default one, the only one in OpenSearch itself.
- The theorems are about one round with fixed answers. They say nothing about liveness: whether some election eventually succeeds when several nodes compete, how long messages take, or how the random delays separate competing candidates. The scheduler theorems hold for every drawn number; the distribution of Java's `Random.nextLong()` is not modelled.
- Pre-voting is not needed for safety. The cluster-level properties — at most one leader per term, committed states never fork, the assertions of `invariant()` at every node — are the subject of the next mission, whose model lets any cluster-manager-eligible node send a start-join request of any term.
